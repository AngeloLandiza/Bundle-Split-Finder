# Design decisions

Version 1 is a working first build. After it come fixes and features the owner asks for, and
then a production-grade rewrite (version 2). This file records what was decided in version 1,
why, and what a rewrite should look at again. Add an entry whenever a choice is made that the
code alone does not explain. Newest entries go at the end of their section.

Each entry: **the decision**, why, and *for v2* where there is something to reconsider.

## Product

- **Bundles first, kept lean.** Version 1 finds Home Depot bundles of RYOBI and DEWALT tools, splits them, matches each part to an Amazon listing and ranks by estimated profit. The owner plans to move to private label; this tool exists to raise capital, so nothing is built for reuse. *For v2:* decide first whether the tool still earns its keep.
- **No modular platform, no plug points for other retailers or categories** (considered and dropped, Nov 10). One example cannot show what varies. *For v2:* if a second retailer or category is real by then, that is the moment to draw the seams: a retailer source, a category rule set, a marketplace catalog.
- **Lowe's is out of version 1.** SerpApi has no Lowe's search. *For v2:* a page reader per retailer would cover it the way Home Depot is covered. Step 6 left it out again (see "Left out of step 6" below).
- **Results in sections.** Confirmed (every part has an active listing and the bundle has a price), Waiting for an Amazon lookup (added in step 5's QA fixes: parts not looked up yet), Review: inactive listing, Review: missing listing, Contents not known. The owner asked for confirmed matches and a review split by missing versus inactive.
- **Ways to sell: a kit inside a bundle is offered sold whole and split into its parts** (owner, Nov 11, "do all of that and give options"; step 6). This replaces "Boxed items decide the result" (Nov 10), which kept a kit as one sellable item and cut the item-by-item comparison from version 1. The owner brought it back in the form of options, not one answer: each resolved bundle that holds a kit whose own page lists its contents gets one option per choice of each kit whole or split, each with its own estimate and its leftovers. A loose tool has no retail box, which matters for selling as New, so a split option says so in words instead of hiding it. *For v2:* the options are the first thing to keep; the labels and the primary rule are the likeliest to change with the owner's experience.
- **The primary option is the most profitable one that leaves nothing; else the most profitable; with no estimate anywhere, selling whole** (step 6, Task 2). The bundle's headline figures, section and status follow it, and a tie goes to whole. Why: an option that leaves nothing is one the owner can carry out as written, so it outranks a larger figure that depends on selling leftovers. A split primary that leaves anything is in Review: missing listing even when every model has a listing, and its status is partial. An option with an estimate beats one without. One or two kits give 2^k options (whole first, then each kit alone, then both); more than two give only all whole and all split, so a row never shows a wall of mixed blocks.
- **Everything counts as a leftover, except manuals** (owner, Nov 11: "everything should not be ignored"). A part with no model number ("18V Charger", "Tool Bag", a blade) is never looked up, is valued at the liquidation rate (the owner's setting, default 0), and is named in the option's leftovers. An "Operator's Manual" or "Operator's Manuals" (any case, with or without the apostrophe) is paperwork and is dropped. A model the plan leaves unsold that a lookup has seen (no listing, review, or an inactive listing) is a leftover too; a model not looked up yet is never one, because nobody knows yet. So are models with an active listing that the plan liquidates because that beats their net.
- **Quantities count, and nothing is merged on a guess** (step 6, Task 2). "(2)" is quantity 2. A kit counted twice gives twice its parts. A kit part with the same model as another part of the bundle adds to that part's quantity, compared exactly, so PCL430 and PCL430B stay two rows. A kit is not expanded recursively.
- **Left out of step 6, from the PO's brief:** one Amazon listing that holds several separate parts of a bundle (the two-batteries example), and Lowe's. In the recordings, Amazon titles that name several models are almost all third-party "replaces / compatible with" listings, so reading coverage from a title would be a guess, and confirming a listing's contents takes its detail page, 1 search each. A whole kit sold as one listing is covered. *For v2:* a listing that holds several parts needs the detail page per listing.
- **No model call and no agent at run time.** The pipeline is fixed and the budget must be predictable. *For v2:* an optional "suggest an answer" call in the review queue, never auto-confirming, if review is where the owner's time goes.

## Data sources

- **Amazon and Home Depot search through SerpApi with the owner's own key.** No server.
- **Amazon first, spread across scans.** Each scan reads a few pages of each brand's own Amazon listings (brand filter `rh=p_123:<id>`), saves model numbers, picture, title and price, and remembers its place. Every other Amazon response is saved the same way. Parts are matched from saved listings before a single-part search is paid for. This is the owner's description, made affordable. *For v2:* with a paid plan the crawl can be a real up-front index; barcodes still cost one search per listing.
- **Home Depot's "Includes" text is read from the page in the owner's browser** (step 4), because SerpApi's product response does not carry it. Measured on Nov 10 in the owner's Chrome: a fetch from the extension's service worker gets Home Depot's 403 "Error Page" (Akamai), with or without the browser's cookies, while the same fetch made from inside a Home Depot tab gets the full page. So the extension reads from a background Home Depot tab (see Extension). Amazon is never browsed with his account. *For v2:* this is automated reading with his session; keep it slow, and confirm the terms.
- **Pace: 3 pages at once, in batches of 6, a second apart, at most 60 per scan; the first refusal ends that scan's reading.** From one tab, 29 of 29 pages came back full at 20 s apart, 5 s apart, back to back, 3 at once and 6 at once (0.3 to 1.8 s each). The limit itself was deliberately not looked for, to keep the owner's session in good standing. A first scan of about 48 bundles takes 20 to 30 s; later scans read only new bundles. The proof in his Chrome read 12 pages in 7.8 s.
- **The bullets come from the page's embedded data** (`window.__APOLLO_STATE__`, key `base-catalog-<itemId>`, `details.descriptiveAttributes`). The parser finds that object by matching braces and reads nothing else; a page whose data is missing or reshaped is "unreadable", nothing is stored, and the scan record says so. Bullet values that are not strings are dropped without notice. *For v2:* this is Home Depot's internal page data and can change without warning.
- **Home Depot's bundle filter does not work through SerpApi** (`hd_filter_tokens` was accepted and ignored). On the site, the "Featured BOGO" page lists almost only bundles; the page reader can load it.
- **Model numbers are read from Amazon titles by brand pattern** (RYOBI `P…`, DEWALT `DC…`). Known misses: DEWALT `DW`/`DX`, RYOBI `RY`/`RYI`/`OLM`/`RHS`. A miss costs a paid lookup, never a wrong match. *For v2:* widen from real examples.

## Search budget

- **Every paid search goes through one client and one budget guard**: reserve, per-scan cap, hourly limit. The tightest wins. Only successful searches are charged, as SerpApi counts.
- **A scheduled scan never touches the reserve; a scan the owner starts may.** The per-scan cap holds across the whole job, including after it is continued.
- **If a number cannot be read, the scan spends nothing.** Unreadable usage, allowance or reserve gives a budget of zero, not a fresh allowance.
- **Known gap: a continued scan can spend one search over its allowance** when the worker dies between a paid Home Depot page and its save, because the job counts only saved steps. *For v2:* count from the ledger, which records every charged search.
- **No retries around a search.** A failed lookup or refresh is set aside and the scan moves on; two failures in a row stop it. The job records the parts it set aside (`ScanJob.setAsideParts`), and the next job plans them after the steps the budget deferred, so at a limit of 1 a lookup that keeps failing no longer takes the only slot every scan (Ruling 50); within a run the order is unchanged, and a job stored without the field plans as before. Pages too (Ruling 52): a Home Depot page whose search failed and stopped the job is recorded (`ScanJob.failedPages`), and the next job plans it after every other page and never moves it up by a top-up, so it runs only if planning reaches it; the job after that, unmarked, puts it first again as a deferred page, so it is retried every other scan. A 429, a budget refusal or Stop does not mark the page, which keeps its place. (A brand page that fails is dropped, 2c rule 3, and is never marked.) A failed Home Depot page, a budget refusal or a 429 stops at once. *For v2:* a failed search that SerpApi still counted (a malformed 200) is invisible to the local budget.
- **The Amazon catalog phase may take at most a fifth of a scan's allowance.**
- **Listing pages are reused for one hour; a brand page for the freshness period (3 days), and always within one job.** Nothing is paid for twice in a job.
- **Saving settings never starts a scan.** A changed schedule starts its clock from the change.
- **Development spends from named buckets** (fixtures, validation, live, demo) with caps in code and a shared ledger. 14 of 250 used by Nov 10, all on recordings.
- **8 searches moved from the validation bucket to fixtures** (owner, Nov 11; step 6). Fixtures went from 15 to 23 and validation from 50 to 42; the total is unchanged. Step 6 needed 9 Amazon recordings (the kit PCL1600K2, its parts, and P553), and the bucket had 1 left. CLAUDE.md forbids raising an allocation, so the controller stopped and asked; the owner approved the move and the 9 searches. The 9 were recorded once and are used from files. *For v2:* the validation set is smaller by 8 lookups.
- **The command-line demo run can no longer judge a bundle** (owner's go, Nov 12; 2 of the demo bucket's 50 spent). `npm run scan -- --limit 50` paid for today's two listing pages (15 bundles) and stopped there: all 15 were "contents not known", so no part was looked up. The command line has no page reader, so no Includes list is read; and its store starts empty, so a title's hyphen split finds its parts in none of the 60 saved listings. The v1 plan cut the paid command-line demo run; in practice it is cut too. A result for today's bundles needs the extension in the owner's browser, which reads pages free and keeps its catalog across scans; the demo bucket's other 48 are not committed to that. *For v2:* a command line that judges bundles needs a source of Home Depot pages outside the browser.
- **Verify and Look up from the side panel spend under the manual-trigger budget** (reserve 0, at most searches per scan, from a fresh account check), and check their whole cost before the first search: a cost that does not fit is refused with the budget's own sentence and nothing is sent. The quote is what the action spends: a part a saved listing answers, or one that wants no lookup, costs 0, and a Look up of only such parts still applies their matches. A panel action and a scan exclude each other, and one panel action runs at a time. No retry: a failed lookup ends the action and its parts wait. Ledger labels are `panel:lookup:<MODEL>` and `panel:verify:<MODEL>`, the scan's label shape.
- **A quote reads the account saved in the last five minutes, and otherwise checks it again (free).** A saved account can be hours old, and its hourly count would keep a button disabled that would now work. The action itself always checks afresh. *For v2:* SerpApi does not say whether its hourly limit is a rolling hour.
- **A step answered free from the cache gives its planned slot back** (Ruling 48; QA 3, Major). The next step that planning deferred in the same phase (listing pages, or lookups and refreshes) moves into the queue, each at most once, never a step set aside after a failure, and only while what may still be spent exceeds the steps queued; the guard still decides every search. A new job also puts the listing pages the last job deferred first, so at a limit of 1 one brand cannot hold the only slot on every scan; before, DEWALT's page came from the cache and RYOBI's was never read. A failed search, though free, gives nothing back. A continued job does not top up its listing pages, but one continued in the listing phase plans its parts afresh and may top those up, within what may still be spent. A page known not to exist (an earlier page of its query said there is no next one) leaves the deferred list too, so it is never moved up or put first next scan.
- **A quote does not look in the saved responses.** A Verify of a listing whose page is already cached, or a lookup already cached, is quoted and budget-checked as a full search although it is served free. The quote can overstate the cost, never understate it, which is the safe direction, so it is left as it is. *For v2:* the client could answer "is this cached and fresh enough" without spending.
- **A query's page is never planned before its earlier pages** (final review, Important). The later pages of a failed page's query wait behind it with it, and page N is held behind page N−1 while N−1 is unanswered (the entry below gives the rule as it now stands); before, a failed page 1 left page 2 in front, and a later scan paid for page 2 although page 1 might have said it does not exist. The same rule stops a job stored before the known-not-to-exist fix (c588c70) paying once for such a page, because page 1 now takes its place and drops it.
- **Page N waits only while page N−1 is unanswered** (F9 review, Important). Sorting every query's pages on every scan read page 1 again ahead of a deferred page 2 and starved page 2 at limits 1 to 3; each job now carries `ScanJob.answeredPages`, the pages whose last answer said there is a next one (a failure removes a page), and page N keeps its deferred place once N−1 is among them, waiting behind it otherwise, at planning and at a top-up, where a page passed over stays movable until N−1 answers in the same run. *For v2:* an answer is trusted until the page is read again, however old, as before this ordering existed.
- **A failing lookup goes after every other candidate, not only after the deferred ones** (Ruling 57; QA 4): each job carries `ScanJob.failingParts`, the parts whose last lookup or refresh failed and that are not answered since, least recent failure first, and plans them after every other part in that order, so at a limit of 1 two failing parts no longer take turns while DCK540D1, DCS334B, DCB606 and DCS565B are never tried; a failing part is still tried when the allowance reaches it or nothing else waits.
- **A Verify or Look up that needs more than the per-scan limit says so** (Ruling 53; QA 4, N10): its quote and its run are refused with `This needs 2 searches, and a scan may use 1. Raise the searches per scan in settings to do it here.`, the numbers from the quote, instead of the scan's untrue "Stopped: this run reached its search limit.". The reserve and hourly refusals describe the action too, never "Stopped": a manual action may use the reserve down to zero, so a reserve refusal is `This needs 2 searches, and 1 is left this month.` (or `No searches are left this month.`), and the hourly one `SerpApi allows 50 searches an hour, and they are used. Try again later.` with the account's number (`SerpApi's hourly limit is reached. Try again later.` without one); a scan's own stop texts are unchanged. *Amended by Ruling 72 (below):* a Look up of several parts above the per-scan limit is no longer refused; the sentence now shows only for a limit of 0, which settings never allow, so the extension no longer shows it.
- **A scan looks up a kit's parts last of all, and only when the kit has no active listing and no open question** (step 6, Task 3; Ruling 69). Which parts: those with a model number of every kit some resolved bundle's options split, that no split holds itself (a kit part that a split holds is that part, at its usual rank). They are planned only after the kit itself was looked up (match exists, not waiting) and has no active listing by the report's rule (no listing, the owner's "None of these", or an inactive listing). They go after every other lookup and refresh, failing ones included, and take only what the allowance leaves after the ordinary plan; a job's start never counts them, so the listing-page share and every other rank are unchanged, and a Ruling 48 top-up reaches them last. Why: before the kit's own lookup nobody knows it does not sell whole, and a kit with a listing makes 7 more searches a guess. **Ruling 69:** a kit whose own match is in review holds its parts, because the owner may still choose a listing that is the kit; after "None of these" they are planned. Cost if wrong: the split option fills one answer later. Free matches from saved listings run for every kit part whatever the kit's state. The panel's Look up on them is never held (the owner asked). A kit looked up in a scan gets its parts planned from the next scan.
- **A Look up above the per-scan limit looks up what fits, best first, and leaves the rest waiting** (Ruling 72; step 6 QA 1). A kit split into its parts can need 7 to 9 lookups and the default limit is 5, so the split option's Look up was always refused. The press now spends at most the limit when the limit allows at least 1 search, in the scan's own order (`lookupOrder`, kit parts after other parts), and marks the rest waiting as a scan marks what it defers. The quote carries `leftWaiting`, the button says how many it settles (see Design), and after the press it is quoted again for what is left. The failed part of a press waits from the failure on, so the next press and scan rank it behind parts that waited longer. An uncapped Look up of several parts also runs in the scan's order now, not the order asked. Why: two presses beat a button that cannot be pressed. Cost if wrong: the owner spends in two presses, never more than the limit. Reversible. *For v2:* a part that failed only in the panel still ranks ahead of parts with no match record; the panel keeps no failing list.
- **The month and the hour refuse a Look up rather than cut it** (Ruling 72). Only the per-scan limit is a choice the owner made for one run. A press that fits the limit but not the searches left this month or this hour is refused with that limit's own sentence (`This needs 5 searches, and 3 are left this month.`), checked against the count the limit allows, not cut to what is left.
- **One search per product: a RYOBI model written with and without its tool-only B is one product** (Ruling 70). A scan plans one lookup and one refresh per product, the first written form in plan order (ordinary parts before kit parts). The panel's quote and its Look up count one search for both forms and take the first form asked; the other takes the result free afterwards. The count is made before the per-scan cap, so the cap never cuts a product's first form and keeps its twin, and the quote equals the spend (a test spends exactly its quote with a twin asked at the default 5). Why: a kit's list writes PCL235 where Amazon sells PCL235B, and the same product asked twice is a wasted search and a second question.

## Pipeline

- **A scan is a saved job.** Results are written to the store first, then the job is saved, after every paid step. An interrupted job is continued and repeats its last step from the cache.
- **Every response carries the time it was received** (`SearchResult.at`), and every time written from a response uses it, not the clock. A response replayed from the cache days later keeps its real age, and an older response never overwrites a newer record. Match records, review items, cursors and the job use the clock: they record when the tool decided something.
- **A job stopped by a limit is finished, not paused.** Its leftover lookups are marked waiting and go first next scan. Only a job interrupted mid-run is continued.
- **A stop asked for at any point of a run ends the job `stopped` with `stopReason: 'user'`, never `done`** (QA M7). Once Stop is seen it holds for the run; it is asked again when the parts are planned (a stop during the last listing search or during page reading plans no lookups) and at the end (a stop during the last search, when the queue emptied as usual). A failed search keeps its own reason (`error`, `rate_limited`), whose message says more; bundle pages left unread are read by the next scan, which is a new job.
- **A failure set aside names what failed** (Ruling 49; QA 3, N4): the job's `stopMessage` gets `The lookup of <model> failed: <message>` or `The price check of <model> failed: <message>`, after any other sentence there, instead of SerpApi's bare error, which read as part of the sentence before it. A failure that stops the scan (`halt`) keeps the bare message, which the stop line already frames.
- **The pipeline depends on three injected things** (search client, budget view, store), so the same code runs in tests, on the command line and in the service worker. *For v2:* keep this; it is why 300+ tests run with no network.
- **Tests replay recorded SerpApi responses** through the real client and budget. Expected values come from the recordings. *For v2:* keep the recordings; they are the regression suite.
- **`runner.ts` has grown to about 600 lines.** *For v2:* split the catalog phase, the planning and the stopping rules into their own modules.
- **Bundle pages are read during a scan, free, after the listing pages and before the split**, through an injected reader, so tests and the command line run without Chrome. A page is read once and kept on the bundle record (a later listing row keeps it); an unreadable or refused page is tried again next scan. The per-scan cap counts across a continued job.
- **A split review item follows the latest reading.** An open item takes the new reason, and an item the scan itself closed is reopened when a page sends the bundle back to review. An item the owner answered is never touched.
- **No estimate for a bundle whose parts are not looked up yet** (step 5). A part waiting for its lookup, or with no match record, has no price; valuing it at the liquidation rate showed an invented loss (the whole bundle price). Such a bundle has `estimate: null` everywhere: the list, Detail, the CSV export and notifications. A part looked up with no listing still counts at the liquidation rate, the owner's setting.
- **A bundle whose parts were not looked up yet goes to "Waiting for an Amazon lookup"** (QA M3). A resolved, priced bundle with a part `waiting` or `unmatched` is in the `waiting` section, right after Confirmed, whatever its other parts' listings say; before, such parts read as availability `missing` and put the bundle under Review: missing listing although nobody had looked.
- **The stale-price line uses the default freshness period (3 days)**; the settings have no freshness field. *For v2:* make it a setting if the owner wants it.
- **Known gaps in page reading, left as they are:** a continued job may ask once more for a page refused earlier in the same job (only if the worker dies between reading and planning); if the save after reading fails, the count is lost and a job can read up to twice the cap; a page message can be replaced by a later failed lookup's message; a bundle page that always answers something other than the page (a removed special buy, say; unverified) counts as refused on every scan and ends that scan's reading; a network error in the tab counts as a refusal.
- **Known gap, for the owner: a Home Depot listing page that fails on every scan still holds up the lookups** (Ruling 58; QA walkthrough 5). A listing search error stops the scan (step 3's rule), and a failed page is planned after the other pages (Ruling 52), but it is still planned. With 2 pages per brand, a page that keeps failing takes the only search at a limit of 1, and at the default limit it ends every scan before any lookup. The owner sees the error notice each time, and Look up on each row still works. The replay showed this only because it has no recording for page 2, which fails every time there. Fixing it means setting a failed listing page aside and going on to the parts, as a failed lookup already is. That changes the stopping rule, so it is left to the owner. A smaller gap: when the one search a scan may use fails, the limit notice reads "It used 0 of the 1 search allowed", because a failed search is not counted as used.
- **A plain kit's own page is read in the free page phase once a resolved split holds it** (Ruling 67; step 6, Task 3b). In a scan of the default searches a plain kit such as PCL1600K2 (317987591) is a catalog entry, not a bundle (a RYOBI model is a bundle only with a hyphen or as a special buy, and no default query is bundles-only), so its page was never read and its bundle would have said "contents not known" for ever. A catalog entry now keeps its listing row's item id and, once read, its page. After the split and before the planning, a scan reads the pages of catalog entries marked a kit that a resolved split holds (the owner's answer included; "None of these" holds nothing), after the bundle pages, newest bundle first. Cost if wrong: more free page reads, within the cap.
- **Limits of kit-page reading.**
  - The cap counts pages asked for: bundle pages and kit pages together never ask Home Depot for more than `pageReadsPerScan` in one run.
  - The reader waits what is left of its 1 s pause before a call that starts within 1 s of its last, which covers the kit pass following the bundle pass at once.
  - Each kit is read once by one digits-only item id, never through a search; of records `sameModel` groups as one kit (PCL1600K2 and PCL1600K2B), only the first readable one in key order is read.
  - It is not read when a stored record of the same kit already has a page, or when the kit has a bundle record of its own (the bundle phase reads that one).
  - There are no kit pages after a stop, after Home Depot refused a page that run, or after the reader threw; the next scan reads them. An unreadable or refused kit page is asked for every scan and takes a slot, as a bundle page does.
  - Reading after the split, not with the bundle pages, opens a second background tab in a scan that reads kits; the gain is that this scan's splits, not last scan's, decide which kits are read.
  - A catalog entry saved before this step has no item id and stays unread until a listing row shows it again. No database upgrade: `itemId` and `page` are optional fields in a schemaless store and `DB_VERSION` stays 1.
- **Known gap: failing parts hold up a kit's parts** (step 6, Task 3; the same kind as Ruling 58). Kit parts come after every other lookup and refresh, failing ones included, and two failures in a row end a scan. While two or more ordinary parts fail on every scan, no scan reaches a kit part (on the replay, 5 parts with no recording do this, so a scan of the full replay never reaches one). The owner's Look up on them works. Fixing it means changing the stopping rule, so it is left to the owner.
- **Known gap: kit parts deferred as `waiting` stay `waiting`** once the kit sells whole or goes to review. The report and the panel read `waiting` as not looked up, so nothing shows wrong and nothing is spent, but an old `waitingSince` would put such a part first if its kit lost its listing later. *For v2:* clear `waiting` from parts no scan wants, or keep the rank in a field of its own.
- **Known gap: stored-data oddities of the kit work, left as they are.**
  - A model listed under two item ids (PCL2001K3N, PCL280K1 in the recordings) keeps the last row's id; a page once read is kept whatever id a later row shows.
  - A kit pass interrupted after the reader answered but before the save sends that kit's id again on continuing; its reading is saved and counted once.
  - A stop message can carry two page sentences (the bundle pass read none, the kit pass read some).
  - A match question's reason is read as data (`NOT_CONFIRMED`, "The listing's Amazon page does not confirm the part"), so rewording it without a migration would stop old Verify reviews being recognised.
  - Questions stored before the cap of 5 keep their long lists until they close (Ruling 71); twins that diverged before the twin rule stay so until one is next written.
- **Known gap: "Look up X of N parts" can overcount** when a product cut by the limit was asked in both written forms (N counts forms, `leftWaiting` counts products); the panel's buttons send one bundle's or option's parts, so it needs a recorded bundle listing both forms, and none does. The twin of a product the limit cut is left as it was, not marked waiting, and takes the result after a later press or scan. *For v2:* count N in products.
- **The Home Depot reader lets its tab settle, and a refusal names what came back** (step 6b, Task 2). The owner's first real scan (Nov 17) was refused on its first page and read nothing; a scan minutes later read every page, and the same in-tab fetch in a tab open a few seconds returned 200 with the page data six times. The likely cause, a cold Home Depot session in the reader's background tab, is not proven, because the reader kept no status. Now the doorway tab waits `settleMs` (2500 ms; 50 in the end-to-end build) after it reports complete, before the first in-tab fetch; a refused page records its HTTP status (0 when the fetch threw or no answer came), and the note reads `Home Depot refused a page (HTTP 403); the rest are read next scan.` (`(no answer)` for 0). A 200 without page data also reads `(HTTP 200)`, on purpose: a check page served as 200 is what the note should show. No retry, no second doorway, no other headers: retrying a refusal would be working around Home Depot's check. *For v2:* if refusals continue, the status in the note says whether waiting helped.
- **A Home Depot product page the owner has open is split and matched by the scan's own steps** (step 7, Task 1). `parseProductPage` reads the page's own state (`base-catalog-<itemId>`: `identifiers`, the bullets, and the `pricing(` entry's `value` and `original`); `notePage` stores the bundle and runs the same `splitOne` and `savedMatch` the runner uses, extracted from it, never a copy, with a client that refuses every search, and touches no scan job. A visit never changes a stored bundle's price, was-price, title or seen time (the page's price is one store's and would flip Deals against the scan's); the page's price shows in the chip and This page only. A new bundle takes the page's price; one from a page with no price is seen at 0, so the next listing fills it in. While a scan or a panel action runs the page is read over a copy-on-write layer (`OverlayStore`) and nothing is written; the visit is kept in `chrome.storage.session` with its time and stored, in visit order, when the action ends, even after a worker restart. A page over `PAGE_LIMITS` (sizes, counts, prices) is refused whole, never cut, because a cut Includes list would make an uncertain split look certain. Home Depot does not state the split (`bundleItems` is null), so the splitter stays the source. Accepted: brand and model always come from the page; a visit can close a question exactly as a scan would; bundles the owner browses join Deals and Review. *For v2:* a page that keeps failing is retried every tick with no cap.
- **The validation harness runs the tool's own steps over a store a first scan would leave** (step 8, `scripts/validate-cli.ts`, acceptance criterion 6). First the scan's listing phase with the default settings (`runScan`), answered only from the disk cache and never paid for: five brand pages (DEWALT 1 to 3, RYOBI 1 and 2, all earlier scans bought) and the default queries' Home Depot pages, which store the catalog the splitter reads and the kits' item ids. ## Matching and splitting

- **Never guess.** An uncertain split or match becomes a review item or a lower grade. Tests are built on real examples.
- **An Amazon search row counts as "probable"** when its brand field (or title prefix) is the brand and the model number is a whole token in the title, and it is not renewed, aftermarket, a kit or a multipack. Search rows carry a brand field but no barcode or part number; a detail page (1 search) upgrades the grade.
- **A part matched from saved listings skips its lookup** even though the saved pool is not the same as a live search. Same evidence, same grade; the risk is a valid but less popular listing, never a different product.
- **Amazon title models may resolve a DEWALT bundle only to a kit plus one item, and only when the stored catalog found nothing for the short code** (three or more characters). A stored ambiguity is never settled by a title, and glued short codes are never resolved from titles; both go to review. A `DCK` model read from a title is always a kit. *For v2:* Home Depot's own page names the parts; once the page reader exists, this path matters less.
- **A bundle is recognised by the shape of its model number** (RYOBI hyphen or `SB`, DEWALT `…W…`); plain kits and single tools go to the catalog only and get no lookup.
- **`DCK…` is asked for as a kit; `DCB205-2` as a pack of 2.**
- **Known gaps, left as they are:** several RYOBI kits are not recognised as kits by the catalog rule; aftermarket "for DeWalt" titles land in review rather than being rejected; `PCL500525SB` (six-digit special buy) has no split rule.
- **A bundle's own page is a second split signal, reconciled with the model number** (`core/src/pageSplit.ts`; the owner's rules in `decompose.ts` are unchanged). Both agree: resolved, noted. The page resolves what the model number could not: resolved, "split read from the page". They disagree: review, with both readings in the reason. A page that names no models adds only a note. On the recorded pages this resolves `PBLRC2501SB`, `PBLRC25B-PBP004` and all four DEWALT `…W…` bundles with an empty catalog.
- **DEWALT: the model at the start of a bullet (`DCF921B: …`) names a boxed item; in `DCK200D1P1: DCD799: …` only the kit counts.** The page split stands only when its parts are exactly the models the page names; a page naming only the bundle's own kit is silent about the rest.
- **RYOBI: an Includes list can confirm a part but never contradict one**, because it often lists the loose tools inside a kit rather than the kit (the plain kit `PCL1600K2`'s page carries the same list as the bundle `PCL1600K2-PBP004`). A model written without its tool-only suffix counts for the suffixed part (`PBLRC25` for `PBLRC25B`). Parts the stored catalog knows and parts the page names may together resolve a RYOBI split, never a DEWALT one, whose short codes are matched by their ending.
- **The page's description is stored but not used to split:** it is marketing prose and can name products that are not in the box.
- **A split question offers the readings the data supports, and only the owner's answer makes one a split** (step 5). Choices come from the splitter's `alternatives`: the model-number reading, then the page reading (the models the page names), then readings the model number could not choose between, de-duplicated. A DEWALT page reading is offered only when the page names a kit; the splitter never adds the bundle's kit to fill a gap. The owner's answer is stored as the chosen parts and applied on every later scan, and only when it equals one of the question's choices; `None of these` keeps the bundle in Contents not known until the owner changes the answer (Ruling 42, below). *For v2:* a "none" answer could reopen by itself if new readings appear.
- **A RYOBI model number that spells out its parts offers that reading as a choice** (Ruling 29). A hyphen bundle the splitter cannot resolve (a part not in the catalog, or not of the `RYOBI_MODEL` shape) offers its parts as written, as one reading: `PCL1600K2-P553` offers PCL1600K2 + P553, `PCL1600K2-A983002` offers its accessory as written. A special buy whose parts are not in the catalog offers the expansion its rule makes (`PBLRC2501SB`: PBLRC25B + PBLRC01B, in the rule's order). Status, components and notes are unchanged and nothing new resolves on its own: only the owner's answer makes the reading a split, after which its parts are looked up like any resolved bundle's. A hyphen part is offered only when it is upper-case letters and digits, starts with a letter, holds a digit and is four characters or more (the shortest recorded part is `P553`; every recorded RYOBI part passes), so an empty part, a bare count (`-2`), a word (`-KIT`), a pack size (`-2PK`), a voltage (`-18V`) or a short tag (`-V2`) offers no reading; `RYOBI_MODEL` still decides automatic resolution. A special buy inside a hyphen bundle (`PBLRC2501SB-A13401`) is offered as written, not expanded. `splitWithPage` passes the reading through unchanged on every RYOBI path that stays in review (R1, and R5 when the page or the page merged with the catalog does not resolve); a RYOBI page adds no reading of its own (until Ruling 76, below: only when the model number gives none). DEWALT never offers its kit alone (`[base]` when a short code matches nothing): a choice is a whole reading. Open questions take the reading on the next scan, keeping `openedAt`. Before, these questions offered only "None of these".
- **The owner may type a split question's parts** (owner's decision, Nov 11; QA M2). `{ parts, seen }` answers a split question only: each part trimmed and upper-cased, a repeat kept (a part listed twice is two of it, counted into `qty`), each must pass the offerable-part rule (`OFFERABLE_PART`), 1 to 8 entries, or it is refused with "Enter model numbers like PCL500B, separated by commas." (`DCB205` on a DEWALT question, N11); nothing is guessed from what was typed. The entry becomes the split exactly as a chosen reading does (notes `parts entered by you`, or `parts chosen by you` when it is one of the choices), and every answer now stores its parts as `ReviewItem.answerParts`, which later scans follow only while they still pass the same rule (stored data is untrusted); an item without them, or with parts that fail it, is read as before, so old free-text answers stay ignored. For a DEWALT question a part may also take the one hyphen form the splitter returns from the catalog, a battery pack such as `DCB205-2` (`DEWALT_PACK`, `DCB<digits>-<digit>`, the digit 2 to 9 only, so `-0` and `-1` are refused; Ruling 47); it is stored as typed and looked up and matched as a pack of its digit (`DCB205-2` a pack of 2), as the splitter's own pack parts are. No other hyphen form passes, typed or stored, and a RYOBI question does not take it.
- **A bundle is never its own part** (Ruling 43; QA N2). Typed parts that include the question's own `model`, compared with hyphens and spaces taken out (`PCL500525SB` answered `PCL500525SB`, `PCL1600K2-P553` answered `PCL1600K2P553`), are refused with "Enter the parts inside the bundle, not the bundle's own model number." and nothing is written; stored `answerParts` that include it fail the typed-parts rule, so `ownerSplit` ignores them like any other failing entry. The bundle check comes before the format check (QA 3, N2), so `PCL1600K2-PBP003` typed for itself gets this sentence, not the format one.
- **The owner can change a split answer** (Ruling 42; QA N2, N3). `reopenReviewAnswer` (worker `{ type: 'reopenReview', id }`) opens again a split question the owner answered (a reading, typed parts or none), keeping its `openedAt`, and re-splits its bundle at once with the scan's own `readSplit` (`core/src/scan/split.ts`), so it is back in Contents not known with the question asked and holding that reading's reason and choices, exactly as a scan leaves an open question (an answer signed for the old choices is refused); it is refused, writing nothing, for an open item, a match question, one a scan closed, an unknown id, or while a scan runs, and match records and prices saved for the old parts stay, unused. Should the data now resolve the bundle, it resolves and the question closes as a scan would close it; `BundleRow.answer` (`ownerAnswer`) says when a row's split or none is the owner's answer, for the panel's Change link.
- **An answer carries the signature of the question it was shown** (`reviewSignature`: the choices, or the candidate listings). A scan can rewrite an open question's choices; an answer signed for the old list is refused with "That question changed. Choose again." Answers are also refused while a scan runs, because a scan rewrites questions, splits and matches.
- **Known gaps in the page signal:** a special buy inside a hyphen bundle (`PBLRC2501SB-A13401`) is not expanded and goes to review; quantities such as `(2)` are ignored. The item-by-item Includes list now feeds the kit options (step 6, below); the description is still unused.
- **Verify grades the listing's detail page with the existing grading** (`pick` over that one listing, `wantedFor` as a lookup asks). Exact or strong: the match takes that grade and drops its alternatives, which were of the old grade. Probable (the page shows no part number or barcode that matches): the match stays as it was. Review or reject: the match goes to review (packaging alone no longer does, PO, Nov 18), with a question offering that listing and the grading's reasons. A page with no title, or for another ASIN, changes nothing. The page's price is saved at the time SerpApi answered. Only an automatic title match can be verified; one the owner chose is refused. On the recording, `B0B3JWF5ZV` becomes strong for `PBLRC25B` (manufacturer part number). *For v2:* `wantedFor` carries no barcode, so a page gives strong, never exact, until a part's barcode is known; the question offers only the verified listing, not the lookup's other listings.
- **The owner's choice in a match question saves that listing's price as the question showed it**, at the time of the response that showed it (`ReviewCandidate.seenAt`, written where a lookup or a Verify opens the question), unless a price checked later is already saved; a listing shown without a price keeps the time its last price was seen, as a refresh would. Before, no price was saved, so the part read as an inactive listing and its bundle sat in Review: inactive listing until a scan refreshed it. A question saved before candidates carried a time saves no price, as before.
- **A Look up from the panel is the scan's lookup step under rule 5 and 2c rule 5, planned once**: only parts of resolved bundles with no match or one left waiting, never one the owner decided; parts a saved listing answers are matched first, free. As in a scan, a lookup earlier in the same action does not cancel a later one whose part its results happen to answer.
- **An answer and a Change are refused while a Look up or Verify runs, not only while a scan runs** (final review). The paid action reads and writes the parts' matches and questions, so `answerReview` and `reopenReview` reply `A search is running. Answer again when it has finished.` and write nothing until it has ended.
- **Review leaves out a match question whose part no resolved split holds** (final review). After a Change, the old parts' open match questions stayed in Review and its count though no bundle held them; `buildReport` now skips them, while the stored item is neither closed nor deleted, so the question is asked again, as it was, if the part comes back.
- **A kit's contents come only from the kit's own stored page** (step 6, Tasks 1 and 3b). A kit record is a stored record of the kit's own brand and model with a page: a bundle record, or a catalog entry marked a kit (Ruling 67). Among them the newest `page.readAt` whose list names at least one model wins; a newer page naming none is skipped for an older one that does; on equal `readAt` the larger item id wins, so the order of records never decides. The asking bundle's page is never read (the match is on the exact model, and a bundle holding the kit has another model: PCL1600K2-PBP004). Never from another bundle's page, never from a title, and a RYOBI bundle page that lists loose tools stays a confirmer, not a source. A kit whose page lists the kit's own model is not split, which is the conservative choice. No kit page, or none naming a model, gives exactly one option. Why: rule 4; the same list on a bundle's page and on the plain kit's page is how a bundle page can mislead.
- **A kit is named as not read when no stored record of it has a page and a record of it is stored without one** (Rulings 66 and 67): a bundle record or a catalog kit with `page` null or missing, matched by the B-suffix rule. A kit whose page was read but names no model is not named, because "its Home Depot page has not been read" would be untrue. Why: the sentence must be a stored fact.
- **The Includes parser** (`core/src/kitContents.ts`, step 6, Task 1; every rule is tested on the recorded pages).
  - A list is a bullet that starts "Includes:" with its colon; "Includes hex shank drill bits …" is a feature line. " : Includes:" inside a bullet starts a second list.
  - Items split on commas, and on " and " only before Operator's Manual(s); elsewhere "and" stays in the name ("Reversible 1/4 in. and 3/8 in. Anvil", 327939252). Two items joined by "and" therefore read as one unnamed item, which undercounts leftovers rather than inventing items.
  - A "(n)" with no comma before it starts a new item (328814689). A list with " - (n)" is one item named by the text before " - ", and the pieces after are its parts, not items (335849279's socket set). A comma inside a number ("1,000-Piece") does not split.
  - A model is only the item's first word: a whole RYOBI token, or the DEWALT leading shape that `OFFERABLE_PART` accepts. Other brands give no models. The page's text is kept ("18V. Charger" stays as written).
  - No recorded DEWALT page has an Includes list, so the DEWALT shape is tested on one hand-made bullet.
- **Known gaps of the parser, parked (Ruling 64).** (a) The " - (" rule works on the whole list, so "X, Y - (1) a" would join X and Y; a bounded walk-back rule exists. (b) Items after a set's " - " parts are dropped. Why parked: no recording has either form, and fixing them means designing on invented pages. Cost if wrong: a future page misreads one kit's parts, and the owner sees it in the option's list.
- **The tool-only B rule for matching: a RYOBI model written with or without its trailing B matches both forms** (Ruling 70; step 6 QA 1). A kit's list writes "(1) PCL235"; Amazon sells PCL235B. Title matching uses the forms `sameModel` already accepts for splits (`modelForms`, `titleHasModel`): one trailing B removed from both sides, so XBB matches only XBB, and `PCL1600K2` still means the kit. Lookups and the free path from saved listings use it alike. DEWALT and the manufacturer-part-number check stay exact. On the recordings the 214 probable-or-better pairs keep their grade and PCL235, PCL515 and PCL660 gain their B listings (216 after the 2Pk rule below); PCL206's only B listings are "Amazon Renewed" and stay rejected. Cost if wrong: an X versus XB confusion for a battery or charger, guarded by tests on all recordings.
- **A part and its twin keep one result** (Ruling 70, Q1a fix round 1). The two written forms are one product. A part takes for free the match its twin holds when that is a listing (automatic or the owner's) or the owner's "none", saved under its own key and keeping `decidedBy`. A twin's automatic review or none stands for a part with no result of its own, so the owner is asked once. A Verify that upgrades or demotes one form writes the result to the twin that holds the same listing automatically; an owner's answer on one form replaces the twin's automatic record and closes the twin's own question; an owner's answer on a twin is never replaced. Review shows a match question while the part or its twin is held. *For v2:* one canonical part key per product instead of copies; it needs a stored-data migration.
- **A spare part never matches a tool** (Ruling 65; Task 2b). The recordings showed RYOBI's own P553 dust chute and a PCL430 backing pad graded probable for the tools, which would count an $11 spare as a $200 miter saw. A title is rejected as `spare part` only when it carries both an OEM part number ("OEM" and six or more digits) and a recorded part name (chute, knob, cap, indicator, pad, sleeve, wheel), unless the barcode matches. "OEM" or "Replacement" in any other form caps a title match at review, with the reason "the title may name a spare part (OEM or Replacement)". A part-number or barcode match from a detail page is unaffected. Why narrow: the first draft rejected on either signal alone and caught plausible genuine titles (a sander "with Replacement Sanding Pads", a battery with an OEM number); a wrong review costs one question, a wrong reject hides a listing. On the recordings nine pairs across five ASINs change and no genuine row loses its grade. *For v2:* the part-name list rests on 17 recordings.
- **Known gaps of the spare-part rule.** A genuine title with both an OEM number and a part name would be rejected unless its barcode matches (none recorded). Brandless "Replacement <part>" titles stay in review, so P553 still asks one question about accessories (QA 2). **Ruling 74:** "Replacement + long digit run + part name" as a reject is parked for v2; the narrowed rule stands, because one "None of these" per accessory-heavy tool costs the owner little. B0FD253MKS ("PCL700B & PCL702B", a two-tool listing) grades probable for each tool, which is the open case of the PO's several-parts-in-a-listing example.
- **"2Pk" is a pack of 2** (Q1a fix round 1), like "2-Pack". On the recordings it changes one grade: B0CRSVC28P, a non-retail 2-pack of PCL660, from probable to reject.
- **A match question offers at most 5 listings, best first, and says why** (Ruling 71; step 6 QA 1). A tool with many accessory listings asked 22 choices, which is noise. Order: grade, then most reviews, then a price shown, then response order. The reason adds "title says Replacement or OEM, which often marks a spare part" when any listing shown says so, whatever its grade's reason, and "N more listing(s) not shown" when some were cut; the brand note counts only listings graded for an unconfirmed brand. Cost if wrong: a real listing ranked sixth, mitigated by the ranking. The Verify question (one listing) is unchanged.
- **Every scan judges automatic "none" and "review" results again, free** (Ruling 73). Before, a stale "No Amazon listing found" or an old question stayed for ever, since no scan rewrote match records. Now saved listings and a twin's settled match are judged again on every scan; a match closes the open question ("resolved by a later scan"), and a question a scan closed can be asked again. The owner's answers are never touched. On a store from before Ruling 70, PCL660's automatic none becomes its B listing with 0 searches. Cost if wrong: one free pass per scan.
- **Product-page evidence beats a search row** (Ruling 73a; Q1a fix rounds 2 and 3). The re-judge once undid a Verify that sent a part to review: a search row carries no part number or packaging, so it graded the rejected listing probable again, closed the question, and the next Verify spent another search, on every scan. Now a result written from a detail page (`source: 'page'`) is never judged again by a scan, the listings the page rejected (`rejectedAsins`) are never chosen again for the part or its twin, and a stale queued lookup or a waiting mark skips it as it skips an owner's answer. Verify reviews stored before these fields existed are recognised when read: an automatic review whose open question has Verify's reason, with that question's listing as rejected. Nothing is migrated. Cost if wrong: a Verify-demoted part needs the owner's answer to change.
- **A kit part's match question reaches Review while some stored bundle's options split the kit** (Task 3). `buildReport`'s `held` adds the parts planning reads (`kitItemParts`), whatever the kit's listing, because the split option shows that part's status in Detail and the question exists only because a search was spent. If the kit's page is read again without the part, or no bundle splits the kit, the question is left out, not closed.
- **Ruling 75: a RYOBI special buy with equal halves is not two parts** (step 6b, owner's real run). PSK1212SB was read as PSK12B + PSK12B; its Home Depot page shows two different batteries and a charger. The special-buy form now applies only when the halves differ; otherwise the model falls through to the later forms as if the pattern had not matched (a catalogued kit resolves as a manufacturer kit; anything else stays in review).
- **Ruling 76: a RYOBI bundle the model number cannot read is offered its page's Includes models** (step 6b). Only when the split would stay in review with no reading at all: one reading, the models its Includes bullets name, as written (no tool-only B added), in page order, without the bundle's own model. "(n) MODEL" appears n times, a model with no count once, one named twice once at the larger count; a count outside 1 to 9 before a model is not guessed at, and the page then offers no reading; a count before something that is not a model never carries to the next model. The split stays in review with the note `page names <models>` (each model once; the panel says "The Home Depot page names PBP004, PBP003 and PCG002."); only the owner's answer makes it the split. When the model number already offers a reading (a hyphen bundle, a special buy with different halves), the page adds none, because a RYOBI Includes list can name a kit's own tools one level down (PCL1600K2-PBP003). Accepted limits: a plain kit the catalog does not list as a kit may be offered its loose tools this way (a choice the owner can decline; a catalogued kit resolves earlier), and a token of the RYOBI model shape that is not a sellable part would be offered as a part.
- **Three more DEWALT bundle forms** (step 8, from the validation pages of Nov 18). (1) A kit name's last groups may be two letters other than W and a digit (`DCK316ST2M1W606`: kit DCK316ST2M1, short code 606 by the existing rule); W is kept out so it is never read into the kit. (2) A kit and a whole tool model with no W (`DCK420D2DCS391B`, `DCK240C2DCS391B`), read with the model's hyphens kept: the tool is matched whole, never by its ending, and resolves only when the catalog holds exactly that model; otherwise the reading as written is offered, as Ruling 29 offers a RYOBI hyphen reading. `DCKTS650D1P1-DCB205` and a kit followed by a hyphenated pack are not this form (never offered as "DCB2052"). (3) A battery pack, W, a short code (`DCB346-3WCS334B`): never resolved by the model number; the short code's reading is offered, and only the owner's answer makes it the split. `DEWALT_BUNDLE` and `DEWALT_JOINED` build the kit name from one pattern. *For v2:* `wantedFor` asks for DCB346-3 as a pack of 3, which a mixed 6/4/2 Ah pack is only loosely; once owners confirm a few pack + tool bundles, decide whether the form may resolve.
- **Four rejects that could not know become review, never above** (step 8b, the validation's `listingsMissed`; owner, Nov 18: catch failures and fix them safely). Each was a rule asserting what the data does not say; each now caps the grade at review with its own reason, which the match question repeats as a note (`isReviewCap`), so the owner confirms. (1) "Battery and Charger NOT Included" was read as a kit by "and charger" (PCL265B, B0BL8BC8MF): the battery and charger a "not included" phrase names (joined by "and", "&" or "or", not a comma) are taken out before the kit test; any other kit word, or any battery, charger or amp-hours left in the title, still rejects. This rule covers DEWALT as well as RYOBI. "Sold Separately" is not read, so such a title stays rejected (conservative). (2) "TTI Ryobi" opens a title with no brand field (PBP004, B09HDZ5MPV): TTI makes RYOBI, so it states the brand, but only as review. (3) A RYOBI title that says "kit" with no combo, bundle, battery, charger or amp-hours (A986501, B09KHGD84Q, a 65-piece accessory kit): RYOBI's model numbers do not say whether a part is itself a kit. (4) A RYOBI title that says a pack of n (PBP2003, B0BG5SPWVL, RYOBI's own 2-pack): RYOBI's model numbers do not say a part's pack size. (3) and (4) need the RYOBI brand stated (field, title prefix, or TTI) and the model as a whole token, and never apply to a title that says "for Ryobi" or "for DeWalt". (1), (3) and (4) never apply to a title that says "Replacement" or "OEM", which stays rejected exactly as before. (4) applies only when the title names a battery (every RYOBI pack model on the recordings is a battery pack, so "RYOBI PCL265 2-Pack" opens no question), and never to a bulk pack or to a model with the tool-only B in either written form (Ruling 70): the part's model or the title's ends in B (PBLRC25B is one bare tool). The kit and pack rules (3) and (4) do not apply to DEWALT, whose model numbers say a kit (`DCK`, `D1`, `P2`) and a pack (`DCB205-2`); the not-included rule (1) does (fix round 1). Why review and not probable: a title cannot tell RYOBI's 2-pack P164 from "2 Pack of P193", nor a kit that is the part from a kit that holds it. On all recordings 18 pairs move from reject to review, none to probable or better (the 216 probable-or-better pairs and criterion 5 are unchanged), and the validation's four parts (A986501, PBP2003, PCL265B twice) move from none to review with the labelled listing shown; PBP004 was review and stays review. *Not done:* the plan's rule that a title starting with the brand confirms it with an empty brand field (v1-plan, matching table) still makes B0B5HR7RN8 ("RYOBI ONEAND … PRC01B") probable; capping every empty brand field at review would demote it. *For v2:* the kit and pack cases want the part's own Home Depot title ("65-Piece … Kit", "(2-Pack)") as evidence, which a split answer does not carry.
- **A kit listing that adds items is review, never probable** (step 8c, found by the audit corpus on DCK240C2; one fix round). For a part that is itself a kit (`wantedFor` isKit: a catalog kit, or DEWALT `DCK…`), a title that sells the kit with more caps the grade at review (`KIT_WITH_EXTRAS`; the question repeats it and the panel says "Check whether it is this kit alone."). Any one sign is enough: an accessory set ("set", "40-Piece", "70PC", "70 PC"; not a counted, powered or multi tool set, which is how DEWALT names its own kits: DCK1021D2, DCK300P1, DCK202P1, DCK551D1M1); or something added after a joining word ("with", "w/", "plus", a spaced "+", "bundle"; RYOBI's "ONE+", "ONE +" and "ONE + (PLUS)" are not joining words). Every joining word is read: the kit's own tools are those named before the first joining word or, when none are named there, right after it; after that a part is added when it names a tool not among them, a model number that is not the part's ("with 5Ah Battery DCB205"), or "extra"/"additional"/"bonus" followed within three words by a battery, charger or tool ("Extra Long Run Time" does not). Tools are the splitter's kind words matched as whole words (plural s allowed), so "Lightweight" names no light; `kindsFromTitle`, which finds them inside words, is not used here. Review, not reject: the grade knows the kit's model, not what is in its box (CLAUDE.md rule 4). A barcode match lifts the cap; a manufacturer part number does not. On every recording only B0722GYTWL, B072B8GHHL and B08P6NCHMW move (probable to review); B00IJ0ALYS stays probable and chosen; the 216 pairs and the validation replay are unchanged. Gaps: (1) "&", "and" and commas are not joining words on purpose, since kits list their own tools that way (DCK2100P2 "Combo Kit with Hammer Drill & Impact Driver"), so "…Kit (DCK240C2) & Circular Saw" stays probable (pinned by a test); (2) a tool inside a longer word ("Flashlight", "Vacuum") is not seen; (3) when no tool is named before the first joining word, what follows it is read as the kit's own; (4) a genuine kit naming its own battery by model after "with" becomes review. *For v2:* the kit's contents (its Home Depot page, or the catalog's kinds) would let the rule reject what it can prove is not in the box and see "&"/"and" additions; and once matching uses barcodes, a bundle page that reuses the kit's barcode would grade exact.

Rules: (1) every quote, after case, spacing and typographic-mark normalisation only, must sit inside one of the title, one item specific ("Name: value"), or the description; a quote spanning two places, an empty or invented quote makes the answer unsure, whatever its verdict; a match or not_match with no quote is unsure. (2) The judged thing is the product sold under the part's model number, not always a bare tool (fix round 1: the validation set holds batteries, chargers, combo kits, starter kits and bit sets). The part's name (Home Depot's title) says what the product includes: a kit's batteries, charger and bag, a 2-pack's two batteries, a bit set's pieces are the product, not extras. A match needs an identity quote containing the part's model or twin as a whole model number (PCL206 is not found in PCL206B or PCL206K1); at least one `contents` quote, and the contents quotes between them must name each battery, charger, bag or case the part's name includes (a name saying "Tool Only", "not included" or "sold separately" asks for none; "charger not included" does not name a charger); `contentsMatch` true, `quantity` 1, `condition` new, no `extras`, no `missing`, no quote offered as extra, missing or not the product; and eBay's condition id 1000 when known. Without a part name the model number alone defines the product, so a match still needs a contents quote: the listing must state what it includes, or the answer is unsure. (3) Packaging is not identity (PO, step 9 bulk entry): a New kit pull, a bulk-packed or repackaged item is the product when its contents are, so PCL660 "Bare Tool Pulled from a Kit" and DCS334B "taken out of a kit" with its blade can match. A tool-only product still includes what its maker ships with the tool, so PCL500B "TOOL ONLY No Blades" is missing the saw's blade. (4) Title checks, from the real searches, only lower a match: "for", "fits" or "compatible" before the model ("Hard Case for … PCL206B", "For DEWALT DCB205 … Drill"); a pack or count ("2 Pk", "2Pack", "lot of") unless the part's name states the same count ("65-Piece" for A986501, "(2-Pack)" for PBP2003, matched by number and unit, so "pack of 2" is "2-Pack" and "3-Pack" is not); and "no" or "without" followed by anything but box, retail, packaging, tag, battery or charger ("No Blades"), with battery and charger also caught when the product includes them. Every check only lowers a verdict. (5) **Kit ruling** (fix round 2, controller's ruling after an adversarial review; the owner may overrule): a listing that names the exact kit model (an identity quote with the model as a whole token, not "pulled from a … kit" phrasing), with no `missing` quote and no denial anywhere in its text ("no charger", "charger … not included / sold separately", "tool only", "battery only", "bare tool", "Battery Included: No"), sells the kit: the model number stands for the kit's contents, and the contents quotes need not name each battery, charger and bag. `contentsMatch` must still be true and `missing` empty. Why: 22 of the 138 labelled matches are kits whose listings name the model and little else (DCK225D2 860158654414, DCK240C2 800845491870), and the word check lost every one. A kit title stating a battery count other than the name's ("w/ 1 Li-Ion Battery" for PCL1600K2's two) is unsure; the name's count is "(n)" summed, or else the larger of its singular "Battery" words and its Ah figures. (6) More cheap checks from the review and the labels, each only lowering a match: a quote cut short before "(which are) not included / sold separately / seperately / not incl", or just after "no", does not count as identity or contents, and a negation after a list ("battery, charger and bag are not included") names none of them; a pack suffix after the model ("DCB205-2") unless the part states that count (DCB203-2's own suffix does); "compatible", "fits", "replacement … for", "for DeWalt/Ryobi", "motor assembly", "gearbox" anywhere in the title; "open box", "untested", "used" (not "never used" or "unused"), "refurbished", "reconditioned", "for parts", "parts only", "as is" in the title (eBay's condition id is 1000 on every recorded listing, so it never caught these); a brand that is a placeholder ("Unbranded", "Does not apply", "BRANDED") or does not contain the part's brand (TTI counts for RYOBI); an MPN or model specific naming the same letters with other digits (DCF911B on DCF921B; PCL206K1 on PCL206 and DCS391BR on DCS391B do not lower); a multi-variation listing (item id `v1|…|<variation>` not 0); a title naming a battery, charger, kit, combo, bundle or carry bag the part's name does not include ("Impact Driver & 1.5 Ah Battery", "Glue Gun with Battery and PCG002 Charger"; "Cordless Battery Jigsaw" and "Battery Powered" do not count); and a tool-only product whose item specifics say "Battery Included: Yes" or a set including a charger. Not used, because the labels disagree: "Set Includes: Battery" alone (on PCL235 147276605796, a labelled match, and 366569621360, labelled unsure, with identical specifics) and "ONE+ HP" (PCL515 920158666573 is labelled unsure for PCL515 and match for PCL515B). ## Extension

- **Manifest V3, built with WXT 0.21, React, plain CSS.** One npm package, WXT at the repo root with `srcDir: 'extension'`. Permissions: storage, alarms, sidePanel, notifications, scripting; hosts: serpapi.com and homedepot.com.
- **The service worker is the only context with the key.** Pages send typed messages. The key is in `chrome.storage.local`, restricted to trusted contexts, never in IndexedDB, logs or the page markup (the key field is uncontrolled so React does not mirror it into an attribute).
- **The worker answers only the extension's own pages.** A message from a content script or another extension is refused. *For v2 and step 6:* the chip on Home Depot pages will need its own short allow-list of message types.
- **A scan that cannot be saved is continued at most twice without progress**, then stopped with a plain sentence, so a full disk cannot become a spending loop.
- **The key is stripped from any error text before a scan record is stored.**
- **An account check that never reaches SerpApi throws the search path's network error** (`Network error reaching SerpApi: the account check got no answer.`), never the fetch error's own text, which can carry the account URL and so the key. The panel's Notice says any network error as "SerpApi could not be reached"; the settings page shows the sentence as it is.
- **Bundle pages are read from a background Home Depot tab.** Home Depot refuses page requests from the service worker, so the reader (`extension/lib/hdReader.ts`) opens one inactive background tab in the current window (no permission needed; a minimized window was tried and Chrome opened it full-screen and focused on the owner's Mac), waits until that tab is complete on Home Depot itself, not its blank start page, checking the tab every 250 ms (Chrome does not always deliver the tab's "complete" update event; relying on it timed out 7 of 20 end-to-end runs), runs `fetch('/p/<id>')` inside it with `scripting.executeScript` in the isolated world, and closes it. The tab id is kept in `storage.session` so a tab left by a stopped worker is closed on the next read. `scripting` is the one permission this adds. The function run in the tab is serialised from the built bundle; the end-to-end build proves the bundler leaves it self-contained. `globalThis.splitFinderReadPages` exists only when built with `SPLIT_FINDER_DEBUG=1`; a test asserts the production build has neither it nor any localhost text.
- **Known gaps of the doorway:** if the extension is reloaded or Chrome quits while it reads, `storage.session` is cleared and the doorway tab is not closed (session restore may reopen it); Stop is checked only before reading, so it can wait for the reading to finish (at most about 30 s); with no Chrome window open, opening the tab fails and the next scan tries again.
- **The doorway's blank tab is sent to Home Depot only once its blank page has loaded** (`openDoorway`, Nov 18). Since step 7 the tab is opened on about:blank and named before it navigates (the chip ignores the reader's tab). Navigating it at once raced Chrome's own load of the blank page: in about one doorway in seven under load, and 3 of 120 in a bare loop, Chrome dropped the navigation and left the tab `unloaded` with no URL, so the reader waited its full 30 s and failed with "The Home Depot tab did not load." (a bundle-page read that fails this way also skips the part prices). Waiting for the blank tab's status `complete` (checked every 25 ms, at most 5 s, then sent anyway) lost none of 120. The server had already received the request, so this was never Home Depot refusing; it would have hit the owner's real scans too. The 30 s stalls in the step 9 end-to-end runs (scan.spec, panel-prices), put down to machine load, were this.
- **End-to-end tests cannot reach homedepot.com:** Playwright's Chromium is refused even for a plain visit to a product page. The replay server serves the recorded pages at `/p/<id>`, the recorded refused page (without its own scripts, one of which loads from Home Depot's CDN) for ids a test names, and an empty-data page for ids with no recording, so reading goes on past them. The production-build check builds into `.output-prod-check`, so `npm test` never overwrites `.output/chrome-mv3`.
- **Real-browser checks run in the owner's Chrome** with a debug build and a snippet pasted into the service-worker console. Claude in Chrome cannot open `chrome://` pages or another extension's pages.
- **The side panel reads everything through the worker** (step 5): `getReport` serves `buildReport` plus the owner's packaging answers; pages never open the store. Each screen is its own component with fixed props (`report`, `state`, `api`, …), so screens were built in parallel without touching the app frame.
- **Packaging answers live in `chrome.storage.local`** as one map from bundle model to `retail_boxed` or `loose`, validated on read; no IndexedDB store, so no database upgrade (owner's decision, Nov 10).
- **End-to-end runs from several worktrees use separate replay ports** (`SPLIT_FINDER_E2E_PORT`); the shared fixed port caused spurious failures when two runs overlapped.
- **End-to-end tests use a separate build** (`SPLIT_FINDER_E2E=1`) that may talk to a local replay server on localhost; the production build has no localhost permission and a fixed SerpApi address, and a test asserts both.
- **A running scan's job is broadcast each time it is saved, at most once a second.** A save within the second goes out when the second is up (a timer), carrying whatever the job holds by then; the broadcast at the end of the scan replaces any that is still due. Before this, pages heard only of a scan's start and end, so the progress row never moved.
- **A one-minute alarm is the heartbeat.** Each tick continues an interrupted scan or starts a due one; nothing relies on the worker staying alive or on an alarm surviving a restart.
- **IndexedDB sits behind the same store interface as the in-memory store**, proven by one shared conformance suite.
- **Stored settings are untrusted:** validated on read, with a default per failing field. The shape check lives in `extension/lib/worker.ts`; *for v2* it belongs in `core/` next to `validateSettings`.
- **Known gap: a network error's text is stored with the scan record** in IndexedDB. Replies to pages strip the key from such text; the stored copy does not. *For v2:* strip it where the runner records the message.
- **The settings page saves a field on blur or Enter**, radios and checkboxes at once; the forecast follows every keystroke.
- **A Verify or Look up quote counts only for what it was asked under** (`extension/entrypoints/sidepanel/useQuote.ts`): the parts or the listing, the screen's own count of paid actions, the scan period (a scan's start and its end each begin a new one), and the report on screen. The state does not say when a paid action ends, but the worker broadcasts then and the panel reads the report again after every broadcast, so a new report object stands for "something may have changed": a refusal from a Verify on another screen does not stay. Otherwise the button is disabled, keeping the last count on its label, until the new quote comes. A quote from before a scan or for other parts used to enable the button at once.
- **A Verify or Look up replies when it has ended**, unlike Scan now, which replies once the scan is under way: it is short, and its answer is the button's. As with a scan, Forget key, or saving a different key, stops it before its next search, never during one: it replies `Stopped: the key was removed or changed.` and the parts not searched for wait. **Known gap:** Stop is a scan's control and does not stop a Look up; it spends at most its quote.
- **Accept all for split questions with one reading** (step 6b, Task 3). On his first real run the owner had ten questions whose one reading was the bundle's own model-number split (kit + bit set, kit + battery), each one his to answer. Review shows, once two or more are open, a block listing each bundle and its parts (three rows, then Show all), `Accept all <N>`, and "Each bundle gets the parts shown. Questions with more than one answer stay below." It sends the ordinary `answerReview` `{ choice: 0, seen }` per question, one at a time, so each answer passes the same checks as a single one; questions with zero or several readings, match questions, and questions whose one reading came from the page's Includes list (Ruling 76; they stay below, one at a time, with their "The Home Depot page names …" sentence) are never included, so nothing is chosen that the owner did not see listed and that the model number did not name. The worker reports refusals as sentences only, so the panel treats three (changed, already answered, not found) as skipped and counted and stops on any other; a test ties those sentences to the worker's. Result: `Accepted k of N.`, then ` c had changed and is/are still open.` and ` g was/were no longer open.`; a failure is appended after the count. Its focus is taken once per result, and the run stops if the tab is left. Each row's bundle model links to that bundle's Home Depot page (the link the question's Open on Home Depot uses), in a new tab, named `<MODEL> on Home Depot`, so every bundle accepted in bulk can still be checked at its source (QA, Major). A page reading is recognised by `pageReadingOf` (core/src/pageSplit.ts), which matches Ruling 76's note exactly; a note it missed would be bulk-accepted, a stray match only stays below. The result sentence is cleared when a scan starts, because it describes questions as they were. *For v2:* the worker should send refusal codes, not sentences.
- **A page refusal is said as one sentence** (step 6b, QA). The Deals notice says `Home Depot refused a page (HTTP 403); the rest are read next scan.` as it is, without "Set aside for the next scan." before it, because it already says what happens next; a failed lookup after it keeps that prefix, since the lookup was set aside. The panel matches the runner's sentence by its shape. *For v2:* export the sentence (or a matcher) from core, as `NETWORK_ERROR_PREFIX` is, so the two cannot drift.
- **Download CSV saves every bundle for the owner's PM** (step 6b, Task 4). On the Deals summary row, left of Sort: every bundle in every section, in the order the list shows them, through the same `bundlesCsv` and `api.download` as Detail's single export, named `split-finder-deals-<local date of the click>.csv`. Bundles not yet estimated are included with blank figures, so the file is the whole scan. Not shown with no bundles.
- **The chip on Home Depot product pages** (step 7, Tasks 2 and 3). A content script on `https://www.homedepot.com/p/*` reads the page's inline state (never fetches), sends only the parsed page (`pageSeen`), and draws one 40px `<button>` directly after `[data-component^="price:Price"]` (checked Nov 17) in a closed shadow root; no anchor, not a bundle, or a brand not scanned: no chip. Copy: "Splits into N parts", "Est. profit", ROI and a chevron; "Splits into N parts · Look up prices · N searches" (`lookupParts`, before the per-scan limit; the panel's Look up still says "X of N"); "Splits into N parts · needs your answer" or "Which parts? · needs your answer". A click asks the worker to open the side panel on This page; if Chrome refuses (a relayed click is undocumented as a gesture), the chip's content becomes "Open Split Finder from the toolbar to see this bundle." without growing. Content scripts may send only `pageSeen` and `openThisPage`; every reply is `{ ok, data }` (the merge found the chip reading bare replies, which would never have shown). The scan's reader tab is opened on about:blank, remembered, then navigated, and its visits are ignored. The setting "Show the chip on Home Depot pages" (Alerts, on by default) lives in `chrome.storage.local`; the worker answers `off` and tells open tabs when it changes, so the content script never reads storage. Fonts: `@font-face` does not load in a shadow root, so the faces are declared in the page under names only the extension uses, from web-accessible font files with `use_dynamic_url`, for Home Depot only. Accepted: the host element and the font style reveal to a page that the extension is installed.
- **The This page tab** (step 7, Task 3). Tabs are Deals, This page, Review. For the active tab: "<MODEL> read from this page · no search used", the estimate, "Costs $X · parts net $Y after fees", the parts (Detail's own blocks), Copy ASINs, Open full detail, or the bundle's Review question; while the visit is not stored yet, Save and full detail wait with "Saved when the scan ends." (or "…the search ends."). No bundle page: "Open a Home Depot bundle page to see it here."; chip off: "The chip is off. Turn it on in settings to read this page." The panel opens on This page only when the chip opened it for that tab. Accepted for v1: without a key the panel shows first-run, so This page needs a key although visits do not.
- **The package for the PM** (step 8). `npm run package` builds the production extension into its own folder (`.output-package`) with `NODE_ENV=production` (under the test runner React would otherwise ship dev JSX with absolute paths) and writes `output/bundle-split-finder-<version>-chrome.zip`; a test reads the real zip and refuses dotfiles, maps, localhost, personal paths and any key. `docs/install.md` is the PM's one-page guide.

## Design

- **Open question for the owner: the forecast meter's order.** It draws scheduled searches, then the reserve, then what is unused, so the unused part after the reserve can read as if the reserve were spent. The mockup never shows an unused part.
- **Detail's head says when Home Depot's price was seen** (QA M6): `BundleRow.seenAt`, the bundle record's `seenAt`, the time of the listing response that showed it. Until step 5's QA fixes the head showed the newest Amazon part price time, which read as Home Depot's.
- **A bundle's open split question is asked in its Detail too** (QA M1), with the Review tab's question component and no Skip for now: a "Needs your answer" row otherwise led to a Detail that could say only "not known yet". After a save, focus goes to Detail's title.
- **In Detail the question is its own section, compact**, between the estimate and Parts (QA N5): it leaves out the title, brand and model that Detail's head already shows, and keeps them in the legend as visually hidden text for screen readers.
- **Detail says when a split is the owner's answer and lets the owner change it** (Ruling 42; QA N2, N3). The split is the owner's answer when `BundleRow.answer` is set. Detail then reads "You chose these parts.", "You entered these parts." or "You said none of the readings is right.", with Change, which reopens the question in place. When the data now settles the bundle on its own, the reopen resolves it as a scan would (Ruling 45), and Detail says so instead of showing a question.
- **"Enter the parts" is offered on every split question** (Ruling 34), after the readings and before None of these: a reading can be wrong too, and a typed entry is the owner's own answer. The panel sends the entry split on commas and trimmed; the worker upper-cases and validates it.
- **Detail names a looked-up part with no listing in its estimate:** "Counts PCL1201K2 at $0: no Amazon listing was found." (QA M4). The `$0` is literal because the extension never sets `liquidationRate`, so core's default of 0 values every such part (Ruling 38). *For v2:* if a liquidation setting is added, this sentence must state its value.
- **Each Deals row leads with its section's reason** (QA M4, M5): Confirmed `N parts matched`, or `N title matches` when any part is a title match (owner's decision, Nov 11: title matches stay in Confirmed, and Verify is on Detail); Waiting `N parts to look up`; Missing listing `N parts with no listing`, then `· N to verify`; Inactive and Contents not known as before. A bundle with more than one way to sell adds the tag below.
- **A scan the owner stopped has its own notice:** "You stopped the last scan / Scan now picks up where it left off." (QA M7, Ruling 36), with no Change the limit link. It is true because set-aside lookups go first, listing pages come from the cache and unread pages are read. Stop reads `Stopping…` and is disabled until the scan ends (QA m1).
- **The progress row names its phase and its total is the live plan** (QA m2, Ruling 37): `Scanning · page X of Y`, `Reading bundle pages · X read`, `Looking up parts · X of Y`. Y is done plus queued, worked out from the job each time, so it may fall by one when a brand has fewer pages than planned; it is never a remembered high, which would name pages that will never be read. Brand pages count as done pages (`counts.catalogPages`); leaving them out made the total fall in QA.
- **The limit sentence states the searches used** (QA m7): "It used 2 of the 3 searches allowed per scan." A `run_cap` stop names the limit that made work wait, so it can come with 0 searches used when the planned steps failed, or came from the cache with nothing of their phase left to move up (Ruling 48).
- **Review's count is `N of M`, where M is the open questions, the tab badge's number** (QA m3, N6; Ruling 40). N is the question's place in the current round: the open questions put off this round, other than the one shown, plus 1. Answering lowers M, Skip for now moves N, and the round starts again once every open question has been put off. A count that included the questions answered in this session disagreed with the badge, and it shifted when the tab was switched.
- **Focus follows the owner** (QA m6): Detail's title on open; back to the opened row on Back or Escape, or to the Deals tab when that row is gone; Enter on a Review radio does not save.
- **The settings page opens in a full tab** (QA B1, Ruling 30), through the options entrypoint's `manifest.open_in_tab` meta tag: Chrome's embedded options dialog cut off the key field. The panel and the settings page put SerpApi failures in the same words (`extension/lib/sentences.ts`).
- **The first-run note says what spends a search** (QA N1): "Searches go to Home Depot and Amazon listing pages and to part lookups. Each bundle's own page is read in your browser, free." The first wording, written by the controller, said only Amazon lookups spend searches, which was untrue.
- **A failure set aside is said with the scan's notice** (QA N4; Ruling 44): "Set aside for the next scan: <the failure in words>". It is shown whenever the last job has a `stopMessage` that the error notice does not show already. The wording is true for a failed lookup, which goes first next scan, and for an unread page, which is read then. Without it, a limit of 1 with a lookup that kept failing read only "stopped at your search limit".
- **What the panel tells the owner links to where it came from** (owner, Nov 11; Ruling 59). The owner asked how to check a screen for themselves.
  - Review's parts question shows "Open on Home Depot". The link is the report row's own link, never one built from the model.
  - Every ASIN link reads "<ASIN> on Amazon".
  - A part with no ASIN, in Detail or in a match question, shows "Search Amazon for <MODEL>". It is an amazon.com search for the brand and model, opened in the owner's own browser. It spends no searches and changes nothing in the extension.
  - The Detail mockup's plain ASIN link is superseded, with the owner's approval.
  - Detail dates each Amazon part price under its net: "seen <time>", from `PartRow.priceSeenAt`, on the head's clock (QA 6, Ruling 60). A price the owner finds changed on Amazon then reads as older, not wrong. It stays on one line in the body face, like the head's "price seen" (Ruling 63): a wrapped "seen 12 min / ago" read badly. For a price seen before today, the part's title gives up about 20px at 360px and ellipsizes.
  - Not done (Ruling 61): a search link per part inside a split question's choices. That question is about what is in the box, which the Home Depot page answers, and a link inside a radio label is interactive content inside a label.
  - *For v2:* keep a one-click path from every price, match and question to the page it came from.
- **Look up sits in the Deals row's right column** (Limit mockup), as a sibling of the row's link rather than inside it. A Look up quoted at 0 reads `Look up · no search` (QA N8).
- **The CSV keeps its machine words** (section ids, `est_roi` as a fraction; Ruling 35): it is for spreadsheets, where stable values are what a formula needs. Copy ASINs says which parts have no ASIN. Step 6 appends two columns last, so earlier columns keep their positions: `sold_as` (the primary option's label, empty for a bundle with one way) and `other_options` (`label: est_profit` pairs joined by ` | `; options without an estimate are left out).
- **Detail: "Parts sell for" is the sum of listed part prices before fees; "After fees" is the estimate's net revenue**, which also holds any liquidation value of parts with no listing.
- **Detail: a part left in review is labelled "Needs your answer"**, not "No listing", because a listing exists and is waiting for the owner's choice.
- **Detail: Export row reuses the run file's columns** (`bundlesCsv` in `core/src/scan/csv.ts`), so one row and the full export never differ.
- **Deals are shown in sections** (owner's decision, Nov 10): Confirmed, Waiting for an Amazon lookup, Review: inactive listing, Review: missing listing, Contents not known, with the Limit mockup's section labels; the Main mockup predates the sections. The This page tab comes with the chip (step 6).
- **A "No, loose" packaging answer keeps the bundle in its section** with an amber `Loose` tag, and the estimate line says loose parts cannot be listed as New on Amazon (owner's decision; version 1 has no eBay prices).
- **Every profit figure is labelled once per screen** (`Estimated profit · ROI` above the first figures, and the estimate line in Detail), never inside a heading.
- **Scan now is disabled only within an hour of a stop at the hourly limit**, with the reason shown as text. This departs from the Limit mockup, which disables it at the per-scan cap: a new scan has a fresh allowance and may use the reserve.
- **Every control that spends searches shows its count**: Verify always `1 search`; Look up its quoted count, or `at most N searches` before a quote; Retry `up to N searches`. Open question for the owner: Scan now shows no count in the mockups, though design.md's rule 4 asks for one.
- **A failure is said once.** The error notice sits under the tabs and is the only place a failed scan is explained; the Deals empty state stays quiet for it. SerpApi's raw messages are put in words (`SerpApi answered with an error (HTTP 404). Try again later.`, the offline sentence).
- **Review reasons are plain sentences.** The splitter's internal notes ("hyphen split, but a part is not in the catalog") are mapped to owner-facing text in the panel (`reviewReason.ts`); a note with no mapping is shown capitalised. The notes in core are unchanged.
- **Off-token colours in the mockups** (`#4A2D00` in the Limit notice, besides the Settings ones above) are replaced by the nearest token until the owner decides.
- **Build to the mockups and `docs/design.md`; token colors only.** Open question for the owner: the Settings mockup uses `#B9B6AD` (reserve meter) and `#1E3A8A` (link hover), which the token list does not have. The nearest tokens are used meanwhile.
- **Ways to sell is its own section in Detail** (step 6, Task 4). It has no mockup, so it follows `docs/design.md` and Detail's part blocks, and `ui-reviewer` checked it (Ruling 31 stands: no mockup was edited). Heading `Ways to sell`; one block per option, the primary first and marked `Shown above`; the option's label, then profit and ROI right-aligned as in the head. Labels: `PCL1600K2 sold whole`, `PCL1600K2 split into its parts`; with two kits `PCL1600K2 and PCL1201K2 split into their parts` and `PCL1600K2 split into its parts, PCL1201K2 sold whole` (split kits first, "A and B" or "A, B and C", no Oxford comma). Leftover line: `Leaves nothing`, or `Leaves 11 parts: 18V Charger, Tool Bag, …` (models with no listing first, then unnamed parts in page order, `×N` after a quantity above 1). An option not looked up yet says `No estimate yet`. (The note `Parts from a kit have no retail box of their own.` was removed in step 9: the PO sells loose kit tools as New, bulk or repackaged listings included.) With more than one option the estimate band drops its own Look up, because each option holds its own. A bundle with one way shows no section. Why: the owner asked for options, and a leftover line and a box note are what make a split's figure honest.
- **The panel says "part", never "item"** (Ruling 68). `docs/design.md`'s fixed vocabulary bans "item" on screen; the plan's first copy used it, and the ui-reviewer caught it. Every on-screen "item" reads "part", including the labels core makes (`report.ts`) and the Look up button. Model numbers in labels, leftovers and the not-known sentence are in the figure face; unnamed parts ("Hex Wrench", "18V. Charger") are in the body face, in the leftover line and in Detail's "Counts … at $0" line alike (QA 1, minor).
- **The Deals row tag is `N ways to sell`**, a plain line in the second sub line, shown only when a bundle has more than one option (`2 ways to sell`). It is text, not a badge. The row's lead reason counts each part once: a part with no listing is not also "to verify" even when it sits in review (QA 1 found "7 parts with no listing · 6 to verify" for 9 parts; now "4 parts with no listing · 5 to verify"). At 360px the second sub line wraps rather than clipping the tag (QA 2).
- **The not-known sentence** names each kit whose page is not stored: `PCL1600K2's contents are not known yet: its Home Depot page has not been read.` It sits in the estimate band, with the model in the figure face, and clears after the read. It stays true when the page was refused or unreadable, which a scan retries every scan; it does not say the read failed (parked). *For v2:* say so once failures are recorded per page.
- **Look up says how many parts it settles: `Look up 5 of 8 parts · 5 searches`** (Ruling 72). It shows only when the quote has parts left waiting, and the button's name is its visible text in Ways to sell (in a Deals row or the band, `… · 1 search, for PCL1600K2-P553`). X is N minus the parts left waiting, so a part a saved listing answers for free counts as settled; with none free X equals the searches. Below the limit every label is as before. In a Deals row the cost goes on a second line (`Look up 5 of 8 parts` over `· 5 searches`) because the button is at most 160px and the row's left column keeps about 190px at 360px.
- **A failed Look up stays said after a partial press** (Ruling 72 review). The sentence is kept with the models sent and shows while the parts still waiting are some of them; it clears on the next press or when a scan ends. Before, it was kept only for the exact parts sent, so any progress before the failure hid it. A refusal from the next quote goes before a kept failure, so a disabled button always says why.
- **A refusal under a Look up in Ways to sell and in the estimate band is its own full-width line under the button** (QA 1, minor; Ruling 72). At 360px the sentence was squeezed into a narrow column beside the greyed button.
- **Review reasons for the new match-question notes** (Ruling 71). In `reviewReason.ts`: "title says Replacement or OEM, which often marks a spare part" reads "Some of these listings say Replacement or OEM in their titles, which often means a spare part." (the note fires when any listing shown says so, QA 2); "N more listing(s) not shown" reads "N more listings are not shown." ("1 more listing is not shown."). The panel splits a reason on "; " and joins the sentences, so a question's three notes read as three sentences. The model in a match question's title is set in the figure face; a Deals row's wrapped "Look up X of N parts" puts its cost on its own line without the "·" (UI review, step 6).

- **Step 9, Task 3: the range is a second line, never the headline** (owner, Nov 18: see every sell price, labelled). A Deals row keeps the Amazon New profit and ROI as its figures, as before, and puts `ROI −6% to +12%` (lowest to highest, the sign written on each end) under them in the figure face, only when the two ends read differently as a whole percent; when they read the same the headline is the one figure and no second line appears. Detail says the same under its estimate line with the profit ends too ("Estimated range: ROI …, profit …, from each part's lowest to highest New price. Reference prices are not counted."), as does This page; each way to sell shows its own range under its ROI. Sort gains Lowest ROI and Highest ROI (the range's ends); ROI stays the Amazon New figure. A row with no estimate has no range and sorts last in all of them.
- **Step 9: a part's prices are a labelled list in two groups** (Task 3 and its UI fix round). Counted prices first, highest first: `Amazon · New` (the featured price, which the estimate counts; `Amazon · New · bulk packaging` when the listing is bulk), `Amazon · Buy Box · sold by X, ships from Amazon` and `Amazon · only offer · sold by X` ("New" is dropped on Buy Box rows because the group is New only). Then a quieter group headed "Reference · not counted": `Amazon list price`, `Home Depot · sold alone` (or `… sold alone as PCL206B` when Home Depot sells the RYOBI twin), and `Amazon · Buy Box (older)` (a Buy Box older than the featured price). Reference labels are ink-2 with no underline at rest, still real links. Detail's sentence reads "Estimated range: lowest to highest counted price — ROI … to …, profit … to …". The ways-to-sell note "Parts from a kit have no retail box of their own." and Detail's "Loose parts cannot be listed as New on Amazon…" line are gone (the PO sells loose kit tools as New, including bulk or repackaged listings). Open: the packaging question still says "Amazon's New condition needs factory packaging" and now only sets a tag; the owner decides whether it stays.
- **Step 9, Task 3: the CSV appends `roi_lowest`, `roi_amazon_new`, `roi_highest`, `hd_price_total`** after `other_options`; earlier columns keep their positions. The ROIs are fractions like `est_roi` (`roi_amazon_new` equals it). `hd_price_total` is each part's Home Depot price times its quantity, in dollars, for reference only, and is empty unless every part has one: a sum of some of the parts would read as the bundle's total. *For v2:* a partial sum with a count of parts covered, if the owner wants one.
- **Step 10, Task 5: eBay reference values in the extension** (owner, Nov 19: eBay is a reference until the judge is reliable). (1) *Where it runs and what it never does:* the scan's eBay step (`core/src/scan/ebayPass.ts`) runs after Home Depot's part prices, whether or not Home Depot refused a page, and never stops a scan: a missing key is the job's `ebayNotice` ("eBay reference values were skipped. Add …"), a refused key, a limit or a failed call another sentence in it, shown as a quiet line under the Deals bar (not as an error, and not as "set aside", which `stopMessage` would say). eBay and the model are ports (`EbayPort`, `ListingJudge`); the worker builds an `EbayClient` and a `ClaudeJudge` from the saved keys. Caps are per job, so a continued job does not get them again: 40 searches, 300 item reads, and the owner's listings judged per scan (Settings, 60, at most 500). The cap counts listings a model was *asked* about; a listing answered from a saved judgement is free and is still used after the cap is reached. A part checked within the freshness window is not checked again; a part left with unjudged listings is asked for again whatever its age. (2) *Judgement cache:* `judgements` store, key `item | revision | prompt version | model | part`. The part is added to the brief's key because the answer is about one part's identity and contents; a listing for two parts is two judgements. It keeps the model's raw answer text and usage, never a verdict, and every read goes through `verifyJudgement` again, so a change in the checks applies to answers saved before it. A call that gave no answer (server error, cut off, refusal, throw) is a count in the part's `notJudged` for that scan and nothing else is stored; a part with nothing judged keeps the record it had. 401/403 from the model, or from eBay, and 429 from either, end the whole step (not just the listing) with a notice: no part could be valued without the judge, and the next call would only be refused again. (3) *Values:* `ebayValues` store, one record per part with every judged listing (title, USD price, units sold, link, the checked verdict, the judge's reasons, the owner's answer) and the value `ebayValue` makes from the effective verdicts (the owner's answer, else the checked verdict). IndexedDB version 3 only adds the two stores. The report adds the value to the part's prices as `ebay_sold_new`, `reference: true`, carrying the value so Detail can say the range, units sold and listings; it is in no estimate and no range (a test compares every figure with and without it), and a part with no value has no eBay line, never $0. Detail puts it last in the reference group with up to three numbered links (http or https only, new tab). The label says "sold" only as "N sold": Browse gives units sold by a listing, not sale prices. (4) *The owner's question:* an unsure listing that could count (USD price, units sold, for a part some bundle holds) is a question "Is this eBay listing exactly MODEL, New, with the same contents?" in the Review list, oldest first with the others. It is not a `ReviewItem`: its answer is a listing's verdict, so it has its own list in the report (`ebayQuestions`, absent in a report built before step 10), its own message (`answerEbay`), and `applyEbayAnswer`. An answer sets `owner` on the listing, recomputes the value, survives later scans while the listing's revision is the same, and is refused while a scan runs, for a listing already answered, or one no longer unsure. The audit file gains `ebayAnswers` (the labels.json shape, `labelledBy: "owner"`, with what the judge said and the listing's page) and `ebayQuestions`; its version stays 1 since nothing was removed. (5) *Keys:* eBay App ID, Cert ID and the Anthropic key are in `chrome.storage.local` only, saved and removed by worker messages and never returned; the state says only whether each is saved. The eBay keys are saved only when eBay gives a token for them (a free token request); the Anthropic key is not checked because a check is a paid call, and a wrong key shows at the first listing as a notice. eBay's token is kept in `chrome.storage.session` (valid two hours, 1,000 grants a day) by wrapping `fetch` for the token request, so core's client is unchanged; a 401 drops it. Changing or removing a key while a scan runs ends that scan's eBay step. Hosts `https://api.ebay.com/*` and `https://api.anthropic.com/*` join the manifest. The end-to-end build moves both to the replay server: eBay through the client's `baseUrl`, and the judge, whose address is fixed in core so no setting can redirect the key, through a rewriting `fetch` that exists only when the build has a base URL. (6) *Settings copy:* two sections, "eBay reference values" and "Listing check" (no mockup; design.md says so). They say the eBay figure is a reference and not counted, that judging uses the owner's Anthropic key and bill, and an estimated cost per scan from 1,700 tokens in and 500 out a listing at the published rates (measured on the 217 recorded listings: the instructions are about 1,100 tokens and a listing about 400); the eval measures the real figure. The Anthropic field is "Anthropic key", not "Anthropic API key": Playwright's `getByLabel('API key')`, which every spec uses for SerpApi's, matches by substring. The button is "Add key": the settings page has no Save button. (7) *CSV:* `ebay_ref_low`, `ebay_ref_high`, `ebay_units_sold` are appended; each is one figure per part in part order joined with " | " like `amazon_prices`, "?" for a part with none, and blank when no part has any. Never a sum: a total of some of the parts would read as the total of them all (`hd_price_total` is blank for the same reason). *For v2:* a bundle's eBay total with the count of parts it covers, if the owner wants one; and the judge eval's measured token counts in place of the estimate.
- **Step 10, Task 5, fix round 1: owner answers, call limits and what a stop does.** (1) *The owner's answer is its own record* (`ebayAnswers`, IndexedDB version 4, key part, item id, revision), written by `applyEbayAnswer` and applied by the eBay step to every value it makes. Keeping it only on the part's value record lost it whenever a later check ended partway (a limit, a 5xx, another model): the new record was built from the listings that check reached. Now a check that stops carries over, from the last record, the listings it did not reach (in search order), and the answers are applied to all of them; a check that judged nothing leaves the record alone. The audit file's labels are read from the answer records, which hold the title, page, the judge's verdict and reasons, so they survive anything done to the value. An answer holds for the revision it was given for; a seller's edit asks again. (2) *Every call is bounded.* A model call and an eBay call each get an abort signal that ends at 30 s (`JUDGE_CALL_TIMEOUT_MS`, `callTimeoutMs`) or when the scan is stopped, so Stop ends a call in flight. The worker binds eBay's `fetch` to those signals (core's client is unchanged) and passes the stop signal to the judge. A call that runs out is not judged, or a read skipped: never a verdict. (3) *A 400 from the model* (unknown model, no credit, a bad request) ends the step with a plain notice, like 401/403 and 429: the next call would be refused the same way. A 429 or a 5xx on eBay's token request is the client's rate-limit or server error, not "the keys were refused", so the notice does not blame the keys. (4) *The job is saved after every call* (search, item read, model call and its tokens), counts first, so the caps (40, 300, the owner's limit) cannot be passed after the worker is evicted mid-step; and the worker's "progress" string (`progressOf`) includes the three eBay counts, so an eviction during the step is not "no progress" and a scan never gives up because of an optional step. (5) *The judgement's key also holds the part's other written form and a hash of its name*, both of which the model is shown: a changed name is a new question. (6) The App ID is scrubbed from replies as the secrets are. (7) *Copy:* our own reasons say "listing details", not "item specifics" (the model's quoted words are left alone); a saved key shows dots in its field and the button says Replace; a refused setting is said under its control, and there is no floating "Saved." line (the page already says changes save as they are made). The eBay line in Detail sets its range and counts in the figure face and puts the listing links on a line of their own.
- **Polish: each eBay link reads its listing's price, and the two Remove buttons say which key.** (1) The links in Detail's eBay line read the listing's own price (`$119.99`) in place of "Listing 1": the owner compares listings by price, and an ordinal says nothing about it. The price is stored per link as `linkPrices`, in step with `links`; a value stored before it has none and its links keep "Listing N". (2) The buttons are "Remove SerpApi key" and "Remove Anthropic key" (their confirmations too), not two "Remove key": a screen reader's list of buttons, and voice control, need a name that tells them apart, and the visible text is the same words as the name.

## How it was built

- **One controller session plans, dispatches and reviews; subagents implement**, routed by model: mechanical work to the cheapest, state, budget and matching to the strongest. Each task is reviewed by an agent that did not write it. *For v2:* the plans in `docs/superpowers/plans/` and their "Where this stands" sections are the trail.
- **Parallel implementers each work in their own git worktree** and are merged by cherry-pick after the controller verifies them. A worktree is cut from the controller's current directory: in step 4 that directory drifted into a task's worktree and the next worktree started from the wrong commit, so the controller stays in the main checkout.
- **Each step ends with an end-to-end QA walkthrough by an agent playing the owner** (owner's request, Nov 10), driving the built extension against the replay server through every flow. The first one, after step 5's reviews had passed, found a blocker (the settings key field could not be reached) and seven major problems that code reviews and per-screen checks had missed.
- **The mockups are the owner's reference and are never edited to fit the build** (Ruling 31). Where the build departs from a mockup, the departure is written here.
